<h2>Research</h2>

<div style="margin-bottom: 30px;">
  <p style="margin-bottom: 5px;">
    <a href="https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fo/glx0uhj428s609aktp2sv/APYL8bKpjhv9hvrtRhW6Ado?rlkey=32llgbdegxki1tecc8kj315xq&dl=0" style="font-size: 1.2em;">
      <strong>The Effect of Medicaid Expansion on Sexually Transmitted Infections </strong>
    </a>
  </p>

  <p style="margin-top: 0;">
    <em>[Abstract]</em>
    This paper examines the effects of the Affordable Care Act (ACA)’s Medicaid expansion
on insurance coverage and reported sexually transmitted infections (STIs)
among men aged 20–29. I use American Community Survey (ACS) and CDC Atlas-
Plus data from 2009–2018. Using a difference-in-differences estimator that accounts for
staggered adoption, I compare expansion cohorts with states that remained untreated
during the study period. In the specifications with controls, Medicaid enrollment increases
by 6.37 percentage points in the state panel. The estimated increase in reported
gonorrhea is 54.69 cases per 100,000 men, equivalent to 16.9% of the treated-state average
before expansion. The estimates provide no clear evidence of changes in chlamydia
or primary and secondary syphilis. Because the outcome measures reported diagnoses,
the estimates may reflect changes in testing and diagnosis as well as underlying infections.
  </p>
